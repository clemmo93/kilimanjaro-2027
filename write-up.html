Summary and shortlist

The best bet for you and Dad is an 8–9 day route (Lemosho or Northern Circuit) in September 2027, with a reputable operator that carries oxygen and does oxygen-saturation checks twice a day. That gives you both about nine months to train, dry weather, fewer crowds than July–August, and the highest summit odds. Budget roughly £3,700–£7,200 each all-in, or £7,400–£14,400 for the two of you, depending on how much support you want.

Prices below are per person, sharing a tent and a hotel twin room, which is how almost every operator prices. As a pair, joining a group departure is cheapest; a private climb for just the two of you costs about 25% more each.

The two things that matter most for summit odds are days on the mountain and a well-run operator. Age matters less than people assume: studies find older climbers get altitude sickness no more often than younger ones.

Summit night starts around midnight from about 4,700 m and takes 6–8 hours to the top at 5,895 m, then 5–7 hours down. It is the hardest day by far. The Western Breach variant, a steep scramble with rockfall risk, is best ignored.

Summit probability: what the numbers really mean

On a well-run 8–9 day climb, a realistic chance of reaching the true summit (Uhuru Peak) is about 85–90%. Operators often claim 95–98%, but they publish no method and some count lower points on the crater rim as success.

Days on the mountain is the biggest lever. The most-quoted figures come from old national park data (around 2006). They are dated, but the trend is clear and matches every newer source:

Days on the mountain Park data (c. 2006) Today's operator estimates
5 27% 30–55%
6 44% 50–75%
7 64% 70–90%
8 85% 85–92%
9 — 90–98%

Sources: Ultimate Kilimanjaro, Climb Kilimanjaro Guide. US medical guidance lists climbing Kilimanjaro in under seven days as high risk (American Family Physician).

The independent evidence comes from medical studies that followed real climbers:

  • A 2022 study of 1,237 climbers on 6–9 day itineraries found 88% reached Uhuru Peak; 8.6% had severe symptoms and 1.1% went to hospital (Croughs et al., Journal of Travel Medicine).
  • Older studies of shorter, mostly Marangu climbs found only 53–61% summited (Davies et al. 2009, Karinen et al. 2008).

What age means for Dad:

  • Age is not a clear risk factor for altitude sickness. In the 2022 study, climbers under 35 had double the risk of severe symptoms. A pooled analysis of 491 people also found no link with older age (American Journal of Medicine).
  • One study on a 6-day route found over-40s summited less often, though 77% still made it (Lawrence & Reid 2016). The likely cause is stamina on summit night rather than altitude. Longer routes and good training address both.
  • The bigger age-related risk is the heart, so a proper medical check matters (see Health and fitness).
  • People much older have done it: the record is 89 (Guinness), and a 90-year-old reportedly summited in July 2026 (Alaska's News Source).

Questions to ask any operator: Does your success rate mean Uhuru Peak? How many clients is it based on? Do you check oxygen saturation twice a day, and what's your turnaround policy? Do you carry bottled oxygen and a portable altitude chamber?

When to go

September 2027 is the pick: reliably dry, after the July–August peak, and it leaves about nine months to train. Late January or February is equally good for weather, but February 2027 is only five months away, so Jan–Feb 2028 is the realistic alternative.

How season affects the summit: no one publishes success rates by month, so the effect is anecdotal. Rain and snow make the trail slippery, kit wet and the cold harder to bear. Snow on the crater rim in April, May and November has turned groups back at Stella Point, short of the top (Altezza). In a dry month, route length matters far more than timing.

Full moon: many people time summit night to a full moon, which lights the path and the glaciers. Full-moon dates sell out first and are busier; within two days either side works almost as well. 2027 summit nights to aim for (US Naval Observatory, Tanzania time):

  • 21/22 or 22/23 January
  • 20/21 February
  • 18/19 June
  • 17/18 or 18/19 July
  • 16/17 August
  • 15/16 September
  • 14/15 or 15/16 October

Adding a safari or Zanzibar: September lines up with the Mara River crossings in the northern Serengeti; February with the calving season in the south (Asilia). Zanzibar is good in both windows (Expert Africa).

Tour operators

The sweet spot for Dad is a mid-range operator with the safety basics included: Kandoo (UK) or Altezza (Tanzania). Paying more buys extra medical cover and smaller groups, not a much higher chance of the summit. Prices are per person, climb only (no flights), in the currency the operator quotes; £ conversions use $1.34 to £1.

Safety basics to insist on at 60: bottled oxygen carried on the climb, oxygen-saturation checks twice a day, a portable altitude chamber (Gamow bag), a private toilet tent, first-aid-trained guides, and membership of the porter-welfare partner programme (KPAP).

Notes on each item:

  • Park fees are inside every climb price above. They are $70 a day conservation, $50 a night camping, $20 rescue, plus 18% VAT: about $1,100 for 8 days and $1,240 for 9 (official tariff). No tariff after June 2026 has been published, so a rise for 2027 is possible (TANAPA pledge).
  • Flights: there is no direct flight. KLM via Amsterdam is the most popular (lands around 20:30 the same day); Qatar, Ethiopian, Turkish and Kenya Airways also fly. Roughly £500–£700 in quieter months, £800–£1,100+ in July–August and at Christmas (Kandoo, Climb Mount Kilimanjaro). Some packages (Action Challenge) can include flights.
  • Tips go to the guides, cook and porters at the end, and are a big part of their income. Typical guidance works out at $300–$450 per climber (Ultimate Kilimanjaro, Much Better Adventures).
  • Visa: UK citizens need a $50 single-entry visa, best bought online in advance from the official site (GOV.UK).
  • Extras not in the table: spending money, any safari or Zanzibar extension, and a single supplement if he travels alone.

Why very cheap climbs are a warning sign: once about $1,000 of park fees is paid, a quote under $2,000 has to save money elsewhere — usually porters' pay and loads, emergency oxygen, crew numbers or days on the mountain (Much Better Adventures).

Health, fitness and preparation at 60

There is no upper age limit on Kilimanjaro, but at 60 the first step is a GP check with a heart focus. Some operators recommend a cardiac stress test for anyone over 60 (Ultimate Kilimanjaro), and several ask for a medical form before booking is confirmed.

Medical

  • GP or travel clinic: heart, blood pressure, lungs, and any regular medicines at altitude. Ask about acetazolamide (Diamox), a prescription medicine that helps acclimatisation; evidence on Kilimanjaro is mixed but it can improve summit odds (Croughs et al.).
  • Yellow fever: UK guidance says people aged 60 and over should not be given the vaccine for travel to Tanzania, because of a higher risk of life-threatening side effects. Tanzania only asks for a certificate if you arrive from a yellow-fever country or spend more than 12 hours in transit in one (Kenya and Ethiopia count). So fly via Amsterdam, Doha or Istanbul, or keep a Nairobi or Addis connection short (TravelHealthPro).
  • Malaria: a risk in Moshi and Arusha at the start and end of the trip, so tablets are needed. Hepatitis A, typhoid and tetanus are also recommended (same source).
  • Oxygen checks on the mountain are a useful trend and safety screen, not a pass or fail test. Readings vary a lot between people who summit fine (Wilderness & Environmental Medicine).

Training (start 6–9 months out)

  • Build up to back-to-back long hill days: 6–8 hours walking with a 6–8 kg daypack, two days running. A weekend in Snowdonia, the Lakes or the Peak District is ideal practice.
  • Three or four cardio sessions a week (brisk hill walking, cycling, stair climbing), plus leg and core strength work.
  • Break in boots well before the trip, and practise with the same layers and poles.
  • Summit night is the real test: 12–15 hours on the go, starting at midnight in the cold. Stamina matters more than speed; the guides walk very slowly ("pole pole").

Decisions to make together

Five choices shape everything else; the first two decide most of the cost and the odds.

  1. How much support? Mid-range (Kandoo, Altezza) covers the safety basics; premium (Tusker, KE) adds a fuller medical set-up for about £1,400–£2,500 more each.
  2. 8 or 9 days? Lemosho 8 days is the classic; the 9-day Northern Circuit adds margin and solitude for a few hundred pounds more each.
  3. When? September 2027 (recommended) or January–February 2028.
  4. Group or private? Joining a group departure is cheapest for a pair and sociable. A private climb for just the two of you costs about 25% more each (Ultimate Kilimanjaro: $3,299 group vs $4,099 private on Lemosho) but lets you set the pace and pick the full-moon dates.
  5. Sharing? All the prices here assume you share a tent and a twin room. If either of you wants your own space, supplements run from $76 (Zara) and $299 (Ultimate Kilimanjaro) to £425 (Exodus) each.

Also worth deciding: whether to add a safari or a few days on Zanzibar afterwards.
